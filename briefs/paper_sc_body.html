Abstract

Does a supercluster hold mass outside its member clusters beyond what the members' own environments would hold, and beyond what ΛCDM predicts for that comparison? We integrate the band-filtered (20 ≤ L ≤ 2048) ACT DR6 CMB-lensing convergence over the footprints of 365 eRASS1 superclusters (Liu et al. 2024), subtract a model of their members (each an NFW halo plus its linear two-halo term at its weak-lensing-calibrated eRASS1 mass, filtered as the map), and compare the excess E with the same statistic on matched superpositions of isolated eRASS1 clusters, Econtrol. E − Econtrol is not expected to vanish: the members' overlapping two-halo terms, the friends-of-friends selection and the isolation of the control all shift it. Its ΛCDM expectation comes from running the whole pipeline on the Websky light-cone (Stein et al. 2020) in 25 data-sized patches through the real eRASS1 and ACT footprints, with the ACT DR6 noise realisations added. ΛCDM predicts +0.11 to +0.20 of the member model, a third of one sample's spread, and the data lie within 1.1σ of it in every pre-registered row. Relative to ΛCDM the excess is +0.18 ± 0.37 of the member model in the members' convex hull dilated by 5 comoving Mpc and −0.43 ± 0.38 in a 20-Mpc disc; the 95 per cent upper limits are 0.9 and 0.3 of the member model for the 365 systems, and 1.1 and 1.0 for the 119 with three or more members. The expected 95 per cent half-width is ± 0.75, so the disc's 0.3 comes from a low draw. The aperture integral itself is detected at 6.2σ in the hull. The result is a bound read against ΛCDM, not a detection: the eRASS1 superclusters show no lensing excess beyond ΛCDM outside their members' one- and two-halo model.

1Introduction

Superclusters are the largest overdense structures in the cosmic web, and whether the matter between their member clusters is what ΛCDM puts around clusters in dense environments, or more, is a question about the field matter that individual cluster studies leave out. The first eROSITA all-sky survey supplies a supercluster catalogue with an X-ray-selected, weak-lensing-calibrated mass for every member: 1,338 systems in the western Galactic hemisphere, found by friends-of-friends on the eRASS1 clusters (Liu et al. 2024). That makes a direct test possible: lens the whole footprint, subtract what the members are known to carry, and ask what is left.

CMB lensing measures the total projected mass with a source plane at z = 1100 and no galaxy shapes. It has been stacked on photometric superclusters and superstructures from DES and the DESI Legacy Surveys (arXiv:2203.11306, a Planck-lensing stack on DES superclusters detected at 8.4σ), and oriented ACT and DES stacks around clusters measure the anisotropic matter that connects them (Lokken et al., arXiv:2409.04535). The thermal SZ effect has been measured between pairs of clusters in superclusters (Tanimura et al., arXiv:1805.04555). For the eRASS1 superclusters, galaxy weak lensing has compared their members with isolated clusters (arXiv:2404.17345), and the members' two-halo term is already fitted in cluster-lensing stacks (arXiv:2310.04387, arXiv:2404.17345). What has not been measured is the CMB lensing of the mass outside the members of the eRASS1 superclusters, read against a ΛCDM expectation for the same selection. This paper does that.

A first version of the analysis compared E − Econtrol with zero. An independent referee objected that zero is not the expected value: the members' summed two-halo terms count a shared environment more than once, the isolated control is underdense by selection, and friends-of-friends picks overdense company, so the ΛCDM value had to be computed. Section 3.4 computes it.

2Data

Superclusters and members. The eRASS1 supercluster catalogue (Liu et al. 2024, arXiv:2402.08454) gives each system's centre (the mean of its members' X-ray centroids), redshift, multiplicity and total mass, mtot = 1.46 Σ M500 / 0.39, but not its members. The member table is distributed only through the CDS, which this analysis could not reach, so the members were rebuilt with the paper's recipe: the same cluster sample (8,910 clusters here, 8,862 in the paper) from the eRASS1 primary catalogue (Bulbul et al. 2024) and its optical follow-up (Kluge et al. 2024), friends-of-friends on 3D comoving distances with linking length l = (n f)−1/3, f = 10, l ≤ 50 Mpc, then, for each catalogued system, a constrained search (up to three members removed or added) for the member set that reproduces its centre, mean redshift and mtot to the table's rounding. 991 of the 1,338 systems are reproduced exactly, none ambiguously; only those are used. The reproduced set favours small systems (83 per cent of the pairs are reproduced, 60 per cent of the systems with three or more members). Each member's M500c and its posterior width are the catalogue's, from the eROSITA count-rate–mass relation calibrated on galaxy weak lensing.

Lensing. The ACT DR6 lensing release version 1 (Qu et al. 2024; Madhavacheril et al. 2024): the baseline convergence (profile-hardened quadratic estimator, temperature plus polarization), the polarization-only and CIB-deprojected variants, the baseline mask, the 400 simulated reconstructions with their input convergence (Farren et al. 2024), and ACT's own foreground-only reconstruction of the Websky tSZ, CIB and radio skies. The Planck PR3 tSZ-deprojected lensing reconstruction (Planck Collaboration 2020) is the independent check; Planck PR4 could not be fetched.

Simulation. The Websky simulation (Stein et al. 2020, arXiv:2001.08787; Ωm 0.31, h 0.68, σ8 0.81): its full-sky CMB-lensing convergence to last scattering and its halo light-cone (the 30 million most massive halos, complete above M200m = 1.6 × 1013 M⊙).

3Method

The samples, apertures, member model, statistic, control and comparison were fixed in writing before any aperture was integrated, on the data and again on the simulation.

3.1 Sample and apertures

Reproduced systems at 0.05 < z < 0.6 whose two apertures are each at least 80 per cent clean in the ACT DR6 baseline mask: 365 systems, 246 pairs and 119 with three or more members. The map is band-limited to 20 ≤ L ≤ 2048 with a cosine taper and synthesised at HEALPix Nside 512; clean pixels are those whose Nside-4096 mask mean is at least 0.99. Two apertures per supercluster: the members' convex hull in the comoving tangent plane, dilated by 5 comoving Mpc (a pair's hull is the segment; median 1.0 deg2 clean), and a disc of 20 comoving Mpc around the centre (median 3.7 deg2). The aperture integral is Kd = Σ κ Ωpix over its clean pixels.

3.2 Member model and statistic

Each member is an NFW halo truncated at r200c (Duffy et al. 2008 concentrations, M500c from the catalogue) plus its linear two-halo term (Tinker et al. 2010 bias, CAMB linear P(k) at the eRASS1 σ8 = 0.88), its convergence computed exactly in the flat sky, filtered like the map, averaged over the member's lognormal mass posterior and summed over the same clean pixels: Km. The one-halo part is 35 per cent of the model in the hull and 16 per cent in the disc; the members' overlapping two-halo terms dominate the footprint. The excess is

E = Σs ws (Kd,s − Km,s) / Σs ws Km,s,   ws = Km,s / var(Kd,s)(1)

with the variances from the first 100 of the release's simulated reconstructions: the lensing in the aperture beyond the members' one- and two-halo terms, in units of those terms.

3.3 Control

Each member is replaced by an isolated eRASS1 cluster (the same sample selection, in no rebuilt system, 1,574 in the clean footprint) with |Δz| < 0.02 and |Δ ln M500| < 0.15, the aperture carried with it so that the member's position falls on the replacement. Summing the shifted apertures superposes isolated clusters at the members' positions: each carries its own one- and two-halo term and no supercluster selection. Econtrol is the same E on that superposition with the same weights, averaged over ten realisations. The quantity of interest is E − Econtrol.

3.4 The ΛCDM null

The whole pipeline is run on the Websky light-cone. Halo M200m is converted to M500c through NFW (Duffy et al. 2008 c200m); the observed redshift is the cosmological one plus the line-of-sight velocity plus an error drawn from the eRASS1 sample's at that redshift. Halos are selected with probability Φ((ln M500c − ln Mc(z)) / 0.6), with Mc(z) set per 0.02 in z so that the density equals the eRASS1 finder sample's and the scatter 0.6 chosen so the median masses match too (to 7 per cent or better at 0.15 < z < 0.6, 9 to 23 per cent below). The released eRASS1 selection function (Clerc et al. 2024) is a function of count rate, which would need an X-ray luminosity per halo; the matched mass-redshift selection replaces it.

Twenty-five patches, the Websky sky read as equatorial and 24 random rotations of it, are each put through the real eRASS1 footprint (15,376 deg2) and the ACT mask (overlap 4,865 deg2; the patches overlap by 12 per cent on average, about 8 independent). In each, the same finder, cuts, apertures, member model (at the simulated clusters' true M500, with a catalogue-drawn posterior width), control and weights are imported unchanged. A patch holds 354 systems on average (311 to 398; data 365), 129 with three or more members (data 119) and an isolated pool of 1,686 (data 1,574): the samples match the data's to ± 15 per cent, inside the ± 30 per cent fixed beforehand as the condition for the null to be meaningful.

The simulated convergence gives a noiseless E − Econtrol per patch; adding the 100 ACT noise realisations (each simulated reconstruction minus its input convergence) gives 2,500 data-like values. Their spread, 0.36 to 0.60 of the member model, is the comparison's error: the patch-to-patch part (0.23 to 0.47) is the non-Gaussian sample variance, the noise part 0.27 to 0.38. The claim is Δ = (E − Econtrol)observed − ⟨E − Econtrol⟩ΛCDM, with that spread combined with the error on the ΛCDM mean (the patch spread over √8) as its error.

4.1 The comparison with ΛCDM

ΛCDM does not predict zero. In the simulation a supercluster's footprint carries +0.11 to +0.20 of its member model more band-filtered lensing than isolated clusters of the same masses put at the members' positions; the isolated control sits low (Econtrol −0.10 to −0.34), as an isolation selection should. That offset is a third of one sample's spread. The data's E − Econtrol lies within 1.1σ of it in every pre-registered row: Δ = +0.18 ± 0.37 (hull) and −0.43 ± 0.38 (disc) for the 365 systems, +0.14 ± 0.51 and −0.25 ± 0.63 for the 119 with three or more members, at the 69th, 12th, 61st and 35th percentiles of the ΛCDM distribution.

Relative to ΛCDM, the 365 superclusters carry at most 0.9 times their member model in additional band-filtered lensing inside the members' hull dilated by 5 comoving Mpc, and at most 0.3 times it inside a 20-Mpc disc (95 per cent); for the 119 systems with three or more members, 1.1 and 1.0. A deficit larger than 0.55 (hull) and 1.2 (disc) of the member model is excluded. The disc's 0.3 comes from a low draw, the 12th percentile; the expected 95 per cent half-width is ± 0.75.

4.2 The footprint is detected

The lensing over the footprints is itself detected: the aperture integral is Kd/Km = 1.46 of the member model in the hull, 6.2σ above zero (0.75 in the disc, 3.0σ; for three or more members 1.30, 3.9σ, and 0.82, 2.2σ). The data's E alone sits 2.0σ above the simulation's in the hull and its Econtrol 0.9σ above: a common offset (the mass scale, σ8, or the real clusters' environments) that the difference removes.

4.3 Hull against disc

The data's hull and disc values differ by +0.60 where ΛCDM expects 0.00 ± 0.33 for the 365 systems (the two apertures correlate at 0.6): a 1.9σ difference, at the 97th percentile, which the null does not share (for three or more members, +0.32 against −0.07 ± 0.46, 0.8σ). The null has no aperture-dependent offset (+0.11 and +0.12), so the difference reads as a fluctuation. It is stated here and not used in the claim.

5Systematics and checks

Member masses. The weak-lensing mass calibration enters as one scale on every member mass, and E and Econtrol move together; ± 10 per cent multiplies the hull's interval by 1.07 or 0.94. The ACT DR6 measurement of the same masses, bM = 0.65 to 0.70 ± 0.22 (our companion paper), would raise the limits by a fifth.

The null's ingredients. A sharp selection threshold instead of the matched scatter raises the null by about 0.1 (+0.20, +0.16, +0.26, +0.21) and moves Δ by 0.13 at most, keeping every row within 1.2σ. A noisy mass in the model and the control matching shifts it by 0.03 at most, as does the model's two-halo term taken at Websky's σ8 rather than 0.88. ACT's own Websky tSZ, CIB and radio foreground-only reconstruction, added on the first patch, moves E − Econtrol by 0.07 at most. A subsample of the simulated systems matched to the data's multiplicity mix gives a null of +0.06 and +0.08, within the mean's error.

On the data map. The polarization-only reconstruction gives E − Econtrol = −0.28 ± 0.84 (hull) and −0.26 ± 0.60 (disc), the CIB-deprojected one +0.31 ± 0.34 and −0.19 ± 0.34, and Planck PR3 (207 superclusters in its footprint) +0.45 ± 0.99 and +0.62 ± 0.73. Random-position and rotated nulls are within 1σ of zero, and their spreads equal the simulation errors. The model with every detected cluster's one-halo term gives +0.43 ± 0.30 and −0.10 ± 0.30, a population control (members of other systems allowed) +0.24 ± 0.27 and −0.48 ± 0.29, and aperture sizes from 2.5 to 30 comoving Mpc (defined after a first look, information only) −0.02 to +0.49. These are read against zero; against the null each shifts down by about 0.1.

6Discussion

The comparison the first version left open now has a number. In ΛCDM a supercluster's footprint carries 0.1 to 0.2 of its member model more band-filtered lensing than matched isolated clusters, because the isolated control is underdense by selection and friends-of-friends picks overdense company. The eRASS1 superclusters match that: there is no sign of mass between the members beyond the ΛCDM expectation of the structure around clusters. In the 5-Mpc hull the data allow an excess of up to 0.9 of the member model above ΛCDM; in the 20-Mpc disc, 0.3.

This is the first CMB-lensing measurement of the mass outside the members of the eRASS1 superclusters. Galaxy weak lensing has compared eRASS1 supercluster members with isolated clusters (arXiv:2404.17345), and CMB-lensing stacks exist on DES and DESI Legacy Survey superstructures (arXiv:2203.11306); this is a bound read against ΛCDM, not a detection.

The strongest objection. The simulated null uses the halos' true masses, while the data's member masses are count-rate estimates. An environment-dependent bias in those estimates (projection, AGN, the members' lower concentrations, arXiv:2404.17345) would enter E − Econtrol and is not in the null. The referee sized it at about 0.1, a quarter of σ: a 10 per cent member-versus-isolated mass difference is worth about 0.1. The consistency with ΛCDM survives it, but it is the first thing a better null should include.

7Caveats

  • One simulation. Websky is a peak-patch light-cone whose matter outside halos is second-order Lagrangian perturbation theory and whose halos are painted with profiles; the null is its ΛCDM, at σ8 0.81. A ray-traced N-body light-cone (AGORA, arXiv:2212.07420) would check the field matter between members on 5 to 20 Mpc scales. With about 8 independent patches, the ΛCDM mean carries an error of 0.08 to 0.17.
  • The selection is a matched mass-redshift threshold with scatter, not the eRASS1 X-ray selection function; the threshold's shape is worth about 0.1, a quarter of the error.
  • The members are rebuilt, not read: 991 of 1,338 systems reproduced exactly, favouring pairs; the simulation uses every system. Members of the 347 systems not reproduced that the rebuilt finder did not group can enter the data's isolated pool.
  • The member model is dominated by the members' overlapping linear two-halo terms. That is why E − Econtrol has a non-zero null, which is now computed rather than assumed, but E alone is not "mass outside clusters".
  • The band filter removes L < 20, so mass on scales above about 9° is not seen; the model and the simulation are filtered identically.
  • One weighting was fixed and nothing else tried. Before the weights were frozen from the first 100 simulations, the fit was run once with weights from 28 (a deviation, recorded); the statistic, samples and apertures were not changed after it, and the aperture-size variants added after it are information only.
  • The mass-scale systematic is one ± 10 per cent scale; the Planck check is PR3, as PR4 could not be fetched.

8Review status

This note passed a context-free referee gate on its second pass (an independent model given only the note, doing its own literature search; protocol in the public repository's blind-review.md): "the findings are confirmed novel and sound if the calculations hold". The first pass, on the version that compared E − Econtrol with zero, returned novel but not sound because no ΛCDM null had been computed; the null in Section 3.4 is the revision. After the second verdict, wording was corrected and no number changed: the title states a bound beyond the members' model rather than an absence of mass, and the "first" claim is narrowed to CMB lensing of the mass outside the members. The strongest remaining objection is the one in Section 6. The closest prior work the referees found: arXiv:2203.11306, 2409.04535, 1805.04555, 2404.17345 and 2310.04387.

The member table from the CDS (all 1,338 systems), the eRASS:4–5 or eastern-hemisphere supercluster catalogues, ACT DR6 combined with Planck PR4 or SPT-3G, and a second, N-body light-cone with an X-ray selection model for the null would together shrink the interval by about a factor of two and test its dependence on the simulation.

9Reproducibility

All data are public: the eRASS1 catalogues from the eROSITA-DE data release, the ACT DR6 lensing release from NERSC's public portal, the Planck 2018 lensing reconstruction from the Planck Legacy Archive, and the Websky simulation from its CITA data site. The analysis is three scripts in the public research repository, each with its usage line in the docstring: sc_members.py rebuilds the member lists, sc_lens.py (prep, data, sims, fit) makes the measurement and its checks, and sc_null.py runs the same pipeline on the Websky light-cone for the ΛCDM null. They reuse the halo model of erass1_cmblens.py, the band and mask handling of erass1_act.py, and act_dr6_fetch.sh and act_dr6_simstream.sh for the ACT data. Every number here is in results-R137.md (the measurement) and results-R140.md (the null and the comparison), with the working note in finding.md and both referee verdicts verbatim.
