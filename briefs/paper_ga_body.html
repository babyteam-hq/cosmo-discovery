Abstract

The Giant Arc (Lopez, Clowes & Williger 2022) is a ~1 Gpc arc of Mg II absorbers at z ≈ 0.80, found by single-linkage clustering of absorbers in SDSS quasar spectra and read by its authors as a real overdensity, δρ/ρ = 1.3 ± 0.3 in the convex hull of its member spheres. Simulations of the same SDSS points have been read both ways. A real overdensity must also raise the absorber count on quasar sightlines that played no part in finding it. We rebuild the Arc from the Zhu & Ménard SDSS catalogues and freeze its template before opening any DESI file, then count Mg II absorbers from the DESI DR1 catalogue on 3,822 DESI quasar sightlines through its hull that are in neither SDSS DR16Q nor the Zhu & Ménard searched lists. The expected count comes from dN/dz measured on the same independent sightlines over the whole sky, stratified by the quasar's g flux, exposure depth and redshift; each template is calibrated against its own shifts across the SDSS footprint, and the chance baseline is the other linkage groups of the same slice and recipe. The hull holds 8 absorbers against 8.86 expected. The fitted overdensity is A = −0.23 (68 per cent: −0.71 to +0.26; 95 per cent: −0.90 to +0.63): consistent with zero, and the published 1.3 is excluded at 3.2σ (3.7 to 3.8σ with the other baselines). Every other cell agrees. A real structure much weaker than published (A ≲ 0.6) is not excluded: the published amplitude was measured on the members the algorithm selected, and without mock-based shrinkage this test cannot split a modest real structure from chance.

1Introduction

Lopez, Clowes & Williger (2022) reported the Giant Arc: 44 Mg II absorbers at z ≈ 0.80 that single-linkage clustering at 95 Mpc joins into an arc about 1 Gpc long, in the background of SDSS quasars. Measured in the convex hull of its member spheres (CHMS), its absorber overdensity is δρ/ρ = 1.3 ± 0.3. The same group has since reported a Big Ring (Lopez, Clowes & Williger 2024) and a Giant Ring (Lopez & Clowes 2026) by related methods, and argues from the FLAMINGO-10K simulation that ΛCDM does not produce structures of this size (Lopez & Clowes 2025). Sawala et al. (2025) and Sawala & Teeriaho (2025) read the Arc instead as a pattern the algorithm picks out of an ordinary point field: linkage on ΛCDM absorber mocks finds arcs like it often.

Both sides argue from simulations of the same SDSS points. Those points cannot settle whether the Arc traces real matter, because they are the points it was drawn from. New sightlines can. A real overdensity of absorbing gas raises the absorber count on every quasar sightline through it, including sightlines that played no part in finding it; a pattern picked out of noise does not. DESI DR1 provides more than a million quasar spectra, most of them on sightlines SDSS never observed, and a public Mg II absorber catalogue. This paper counts the absorbers on those sightlines inside the Arc's frozen template. A context-free referee's literature search found no prior work that re-counts absorbers on independent sightlines.

2Data

SDSS absorbers. The JHU-SDSS Mg II catalogues of Zhu & Ménard (2013), in their trimmed DR7 and DR12 versions (35,752 and 41,895 absorbers), with their lists of searched quasars. 8,441 DR7 absorbers duplicate a DR12 one (sightline within 1.5″, |Δz| < 0.005) and are dropped, leaving 69,206.

DESI sightlines and absorbers. The DESI DR1 Mg II absorber value-added catalogue (DESI Collaboration 2025; method of Napolitano et al. 2023) and the DR1 quasar catalogue it searched (the main-dark afterburner catalogue, 1,556,507 quasars, one row per target). A sightline's depth is its TSNR2_QSO summed over its tiles, which equals the catalogue's coadded value for 98.9 per cent of quasars with an absorber.

SDSS exclusion. SDSS DR16Q (Lyke et al. 2020) and the Zhu & Ménard searched lists. 322,606 DESI quasars within 1.5″ of either are removed, leaving 1,233,901 independent sightlines.

3Method

The template, samples, statistic, nulls and primary cell were fixed in writing before the Arc's observed count was computed, and nothing was changed after it.

3.1 The frozen template

The Arc is rebuilt with the discovery recipe from the merged Zhu & Ménard catalogue, before any DESI file was opened: the slice z = 0.802 ± 0.060 (4,752 absorbers), single linkage at 95 Mpc in a flat cosmology with Ωm 0.27, h 0.7. The slice splits into 1,322 groups. The Arc's group has 46 members (the paper has 44), RA 205.0–224.6°, Dec 36.2–42.2°, z 0.745–0.860, member W2796 median 0.82 Å. It is not the slice's largest group: the six largest have 88 to 131 members. Four cells are defined on it: the CHMS (the convex hull of spheres of radius half the mean minimum-spanning-tree edge, 30.4 Mpc; V(CHMS ∩ slice) = 3.5 × 107 Mpc3) and the union of member spheres of radius 47.5, 70 and 95 Mpc, each cut to the slice. The members are frozen in r155_templates.json.

3.2 Sightlines, path and equivalent width

The DESI catalogue does not search the Lyα forest (its dN/dz is exactly zero there), so a sightline's path starts at zabs = 1250 (1 + zq)/2796 − 1 and ends 5,000 km s−1 below the quasar. Its intersection with each cell is computed exactly (ray–sphere and ray–hull). DESI sightlines within 200 proper kpc of a member's own sightline at the member's redshift would be dropped as re-detections of the same halo; none fall that close, and radii of 0 and 500 kpc give identical counts. On the 21,789 absorbers both catalogues hold (re-observed SDSS sightlines), DESI EW2796 / Zhu & Ménard REW = 1.03 (16–84 per cent: 0.85–1.21), flat in EW, so the cuts W2796 > 0.3 and 0.6 Å on the SDSS scale are 0.309 and 0.618 Å on DESI's. 92 per cent of DR1's W > 0.3 Å detections lie above 0.6 Å, so the DESI sample's EW mix is close to the members'.

3.3 The expected count

DR1's Mg II detection rate is set by the quasar's flux, not mainly by the exposure depth. All-sky dN/dz at z = 0.8 (W > 0.3 Å) runs from 0.00004 to 0.28 across g-flux quintiles, against 0.052 to 0.090 across TSNR2 tertiles. Removing SDSS quasars removes the brighter ones where SDSS observed, so inside the SDSS footprint the independent sightlines are fainter than average: through the Arc, 8 per cent sit in the brightest g quintile against 20 per cent sky-wide. A flat dN/dz would overpredict the Arc's expectation about twofold. dN/dz is therefore measured per stratum (TSNR2 tertile × 12 g-flux bins, finest at the bright end, × 4 quasar-redshift bins) on all independent sightlines, and the expected count is the sum over a cell's sightlines of path times its stratum's dN/dz. The all-sky check in the slice gives 7,844 expected against 7,864 observed. The excess is δ = Nobs/Nexp − 1, the maximum-likelihood Poisson excess.

3.4 Calibration and the chance baseline

Shift null. Each template is referred to its own shift null: the same shape rotated rigidly to random places in the SDSS footprint (more than 30 per cent SDSS quasars among DESI quasars within 1° of its members; the Arc has 39 to 53 per cent), not overlapping the original, with Nexp within 0.75 to 1.33 times its own. About 150 shifts are kept per cell for the Arc. The calibrated excess is δcal = δ − mean(shift), and the shift spread at the Arc's depth (Poisson, coverage and large-scale structure together) is its error. The shift means are −0.07 to −0.11, a residual offset of the completeness model that this removes.

Chance baseline. A pattern drawn from ordinary clustered absorbers should still show the small excess any group of clustered absorbers shows on new sightlines. The baseline is the other single-linkage groups from the same slice and recipe, matched to the Arc in size (23 to 92 members) and extent (0.5 to 2 times its 742 Mpc largest separation): 18 groups, 15 with DESI coverage. A group counts only if at least 20 of its own shifts match it, which keeps 4 or 5 per cell; the baseline is their mean δcal (CHMS: +0.22 ± 0.34). The claim is Δ = δcal(Arc) − baseline, with the Arc's shift-null percentiles and the baseline's standard error combined in quadrature.

3.5 The real-structure prediction and the primary cell

An overdensity A uniform in CHMS ∩ slice raises a cell's expectation by A fH, where fH is the fraction of the cell's expected count lying in CHMS ∩ slice: 0.98 for the CHMS, 0.88, 0.73 and 0.52 for the spheres of 47.5, 70 and 95 Mpc (the larger spheres reach z 0.70–0.90 and far past the hull, and dilute it). The fitted amplitude is A = Δ/fH, so A = 1.3 is the published reading, A = 0 is chance, and an intermediate value reads as a real structure weaker than its selected members imply. Putting the same excess only in the member spheres changes the predictions by less than 0.1, except at R = 47.5 Mpc. Computed through the real sightlines before the measurement (r155_predict.json), the separation of A = 1.3 from zero at the Arc's depth is 2.3, 2.8 and 2.5σ for the spheres and 3.4σ for the CHMS. The primary cell is the CHMS at W > 0.3 Å; the others are reported.

3.6 Method check and disclosure

Before any count was made, the plan alone went to a fresh, context-free referee model as a method check. It returned “fixable” with six points, and each was closed before the Arc's count was computed: (1) forward-model the real-structure prediction through the real DESI sightlines and re-pick the primary cell from it (Section 3.5; the primary cell moved from R = 95 Mpc to the CHMS); (2) replace the random-46 clustering null with a linkage-based chance baseline (the matched groups of Section 3.4; clustered mocks were not built, and the random-46 null stays as a secondary check); (3) give an intermediate result a defined reading (closed by reading the result as the amplitude A, not by mocks: how much shrinkage selection alone produces is not modelled, which Section 6 states as the limit); (4) refer each template to its own shift null so the calibration offset cancels; (5) exclude DESI sightlines close to member sightlines, and vary the radius; (6) check the EW mix (W > 0.6 Å as the amplitude check). The referee's other points needed no change: Poisson over-dispersion (the shift null captures it), the fixed cosmology and the template freeze.

Disclosure. The proposal that suggested this test came with an unblinded rough count, made at proposal time and seen in the brief: δ = −0.16, −0.28 and −0.29 at R = 47.5, 70 and 95 Mpc, with partial SDSS exclusion, a flat dN/dz and no EW matching. The plan was fixed without reference to it and was not changed after it. That count was biased low by the completeness gradient of Section 3.3, so its negative sign carried no information.

4.1 The primary cell

In its own hull the Arc carries no excess on new sightlines. The CHMS holds 8 DR1 absorbers on 3,822 independent sightlines against 8.86 expected (δ = −0.10). Calibrated against its shifts (−0.10 ± 0.34) and the chance baseline (+0.22 ± 0.34), Δ = −0.22 (68 per cent: −0.69 to +0.25; 95 per cent: −0.88 to +0.62), and

A = −0.23  (68 %: −0.71 to +0.26;  95 %: −0.90 to +0.63)(1)

That is consistent with zero (−0.5σ) and 3.2σ below the published 1.3. In plain Poisson terms, a real δ = 1.3 in the hull predicts about 19 absorbers where 8 are seen.

4.2 The other cells

No cell shows an excess. The cells forecast before the measurement to separate the two readings at 2.8σ or more (the CHMS at both EW cuts, and R = 70 Mpc) all exclude A = 1.3 at 2.9σ or more; the spheres at 47.5 and 95 Mpc, forecast at 2.3 and 2.5σ, exclude it at 2.3 and 2.2σ. Among the calibrated linkage groups the Arc ranks at or below the median in every cell (rank 0.20 to 0.50).

5Checks

Baseline choice. The fixed rule kept only 4 or 5 groups, because the groups whose own shifts never matched (0 of up to 1,500 tries) are the well-covered ones outside the SDSS-thinned area. With all 14 covered groups' raw δ as the baseline (+0.13 ± 0.13), the CHMS gives Δ = −0.13 ± 0.37, A = −0.13, and the published value is excluded at 3.8σ. With no baseline at all, A = 0.00 and the exclusion is 3.7σ. This sensitivity was not pre-fixed; it is reported because the fixed rule kept so few groups.

Ordinary clustering. The proposal's random-46 clustering null (spheres on random sets of 46 slice absorbers) sits +0.14, +0.07 and +0.06 above random positions at R = 47.5, 70 and 95 Mpc, small next to the 1.3 being tested. The linkage groups as a set show a small positive excess on new sightlines (raw mean δ +0.13 ± 0.13 in their CHMS, −0.04 ± 0.08 at R = 95 Mpc), as clustered absorbers should.

EW cut, weighting, exclusion radius. W > 0.6 Å gives A = −0.10 (95 per cent: −0.79 to +0.70). A literal TSNR2-weighted ratio gives δ = −0.19 in the primary cell. Exclusion radii of 0, 200 and 500 proper kpc give identical counts in every cell.

6Discussion

The step the two readings dispute is whether the Arc's points trace real matter. New sightlines answer that directly: at the published amplitude the Arc's hull would hold about 19 DR1 absorbers, and it holds 8, the number an average patch of sky at the same depth holds. The result agrees with the reading that the arc is an algorithmic pattern among ordinary absorbers (Sawala et al. 2025; Sawala & Teeriaho 2025), and it differs from the simulations behind that reading in measuring the real sky on data the pattern was not drawn from. It does not touch the FLAMINGO-10K argument (Lopez & Clowes 2025) about whether ΛCDM allows such structures; it removes the observational case that this one exists at δρ/ρ ≈ 1.3. It does not show that no structure exists there: whatever the Arc's 46 SDSS absorbers trace, it is not an overdensity of Mg II absorbers at the published level.

The strongest objection. The published 1.3 was measured on the members the algorithm selected, so even a real structure would come back weaker on new sightlines: the noisiest members are not re-found. The test therefore falsifies the published amplitude, but it cannot tell a modest real structure (A ≲ 0.6) from a chance pattern without modelling that selection shrinkage on clustered mocks. The measured A is close to zero, so this bears on the upper end of the interval, not on the exclusion of 1.3.

7Caveats

  • The selection shrinkage is not modelled, so an intermediate amplitude could not be split into “real” and “inflated by selection”; A ≲ 0.6 is allowed.
  • The chance baseline is 4 or 5 calibrated linkage groups (standard error 0.34 in the CHMS), not linkage run on clustered lognormal or N-body mocks. The conclusion holds with all three baselines of Section 5.
  • The expected count is small (8.9), and the SDSS exclusion leaves the Arc's sightlines faint. The completeness strata are a model; the all-sky check and the shift null test it, and the residual offset of −0.07 to −0.11 is calibrated out per template.
  • One DESI release. The Big Ring and the Giant Ring are not tested: neither paper gives a member list or a reproducible membership recipe, and a template frozen from them would be ours, not theirs.
  • The rebuilt Arc has 46 members, the published one 44; the template is ours, frozen from the published recipe.

8Review status

The plan passed a blind method check as “fixable”, and its six points were closed before measuring (Section 3.6). The finished note then passed a context-free referee gate on its first pass (an independent model given only the note, doing its own literature search; protocol in the public repository's blind-review.md): “the findings are confirmed novel and sound if the calculations hold”. No revision was made after it. The strongest objection is the one in Section 6. The closest work the referee found re-counts no absorbers on independent sightlines: arXiv:2201.06875, 2402.07591, 2604.17534, 2504.14940, 2502.03515 and 2505.11072, with 2604.13162 related only.

DESI DR2, with about twice the sightlines, would bring the error on A in the CHMS from about 0.37 to about 0.26. Linkage on lognormal absorber mocks would replace the 4-or-5-group baseline and give the selection shrinkage, which would let the test discriminate A ≈ 0.3–0.6 from zero. The Big Ring is the next test once its member list is public.

9Reproducibility

All data are public: the Zhu & Ménard catalogues and searched-quasar lists from the JHU-SDSS absorber archive, the DESI DR1 Mg II value-added catalogue and quasar catalogue from data.desi.lbl.gov, and SDSS DR16Q from the SDSS science archive. The analysis is in the public research repository: r155_template.py rebuilds and freezes the Arc (r155_templates.json), r155_sightlines.py builds the DESI sightlines and absorbers and removes the SDSS ones, r155_core.py computes paths, completeness strata and counts, r155_run.py runs the feasibility step and the nulls, and r155_measure.py (predict, measure) makes the prediction (r155_predict.json) and the measurement, with the cosmology and matching in r155_common.py. The plan as the method check saw it is r155_plan.md. Every number here is in results-R155.md, with the working note and both referee verdicts verbatim in finding.md.

Methods and code. How this paper was chosen, refereed and recorded: the methods note. Scripts, results and referee verdicts: github.com/babyteam-hq/cosmo-discovery.
