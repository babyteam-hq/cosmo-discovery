Abstract

The Giant Arc (Lopez, Clowes & Williger 2022) is a ~1 Gpc group of Mg II absorbers at z ≈ 0.80, found by single-linkage clustering (95 Mpc) of absorbers in SDSS quasar spectra, with δρ/ρ = 1.3 ± 0.3 measured on its own members inside the convex hull of its member spheres. Simulations of the same SDSS points have been read both ways, as a real structure and as a pattern the algorithm picks out of ordinary clustering. A real overdensity must also raise the absorber count on quasar sightlines that played no part in finding it. We rebuild the Arc from the Zhu & Ménard catalogues (46 members; our member overdensity 1.32), freeze its hull, and count DESI DR1 Mg II absorbers on 3,822 DESI quasar sightlines through it that are in neither SDSS DR16Q nor the Zhu & Ménard searched lists: 8 against 8.86 expected from dN/dz stratified by quasar flux, exposure depth and redshift on all 1,233,901 independent sightlines (δ = −0.10). A member-measured overdensity is inflated by selection, so the published 1.3 is not what a real structure would show on new sightlines. We measure what it would show by injecting real overdensities into the real SDSS catalogue at 200 places in its footprint, at amplitudes 0 to 4, re-running the recipe, and keeping the recoveries that look like the Arc; for each, the share of the recovered hull inside the injected structure and the chance part of a recovered hull are measured on real DESI data, and the hull's count is compared at count level with clustering scatter. A real Arc, uniform in its hull, predicts δ = +0.62 (68 per cent: +0.25 to +1.05): the observed count is 1.3σ below it (P = 0.10) and 0.5σ below chance (+0.15), a likelihood ratio of 0.37 for real against chance. A clumpy or more diffuse shape, completeness-weighted injection, a W > 0.6 Å cut and a DESI-to-SDSS bias ratio of 0.25 to 1.25 give 0.6 to 1.4σ. With one DESI release and about nine expected absorbers, the data cannot yet tell a real Giant Arc from chance; DESI DR2, with about twice the sightlines, would put the two readings about 2σ apart if the count held. Filament-shaped injections, the shape the discoverers describe, were not run.

1Introduction

Lopez, Clowes & Williger (2022) reported the Giant Arc: 44 Mg II absorbers at z ≈ 0.80 that single-linkage clustering at 95 Mpc joins into an arc about 1 Gpc long, in the background of SDSS quasars. Measured in the convex hull of its member spheres (CHMS), its absorber overdensity is δρ/ρ = 1.3 ± 0.3. The same group has since reported a Big Ring (Lopez, Clowes & Williger 2024) and a Giant Ring (Lopez & Clowes 2026) by related methods, and argues from the FLAMINGO-10K simulation that ΛCDM does not produce structures of this size (Lopez & Clowes 2025). Sawala et al. (2025) and Sawala & Teeriaho (2025) read the Arc instead as a pattern the algorithm picks out of an ordinary point field: linkage on ΛCDM absorber mocks finds arcs like it often.

Both sides argue from simulations of the same SDSS points, and those points cannot settle whether the Arc traces real matter, because they are the points it was drawn from. New sightlines can. A real overdensity of absorbing gas raises the absorber count on every quasar sightline through it, including sightlines that played no part in finding it; a pattern picked out of noise does not. DESI DR1 provides more than a million quasar spectra, most on sightlines SDSS never observed, and a public Mg II absorber catalogue.

The question this paper answers is what that count can decide. A first version of this note (2 November) compared the count with the published 1.3 and excluded it at 3.2σ. That comparison does not test whether the Arc is real: an overdensity measured on the members a clustering algorithm picked is pushed up by the upward fluctuations that put them there, so even a real structure would come back lower on new sightlines (selection shrinkage), and the hull the algorithm draws need not sit where the structure is. This revision measures the shrinkage on the real SDSS catalogue and asks how far a real Arc's prediction on new sightlines sits from chance.

2Data

SDSS absorbers. The JHU-SDSS Mg II catalogues of Zhu & Ménard (2013), in their trimmed DR7 and DR12 versions (35,752 and 41,895 absorbers), with their lists of searched quasars (129,410 sightlines, de-duplicated at 1.5″). 8,441 DR7 absorbers duplicate a DR12 one (sightline within 1.5″, |Δz| < 0.005) and are dropped, leaving 69,206.

DESI sightlines and absorbers. The DESI DR1 Mg II absorber value-added catalogue (DESI Collaboration 2025; method of Napolitano et al. 2023) and the DR1 quasar catalogue it searched (the main-dark afterburner catalogue, 1,556,507 quasars). A sightline's depth is its TSNR2_QSO summed over its tiles.

SDSS exclusion. SDSS DR16Q (Lyke et al. 2020) and the Zhu & Ménard searched lists. 322,606 DESI quasars within 1.5″ of either are removed, leaving 1,233,901 independent sightlines.

3Method

3.1 The frozen template and the DESI count

The Arc is rebuilt with the discovery recipe before any DESI file was opened: the slice z = 0.802 ± 0.060 (4,752 absorbers), single linkage at 95 Mpc in a flat cosmology with Ωm 0.27, h 0.7. The Arc's group has 46 members (the paper has 44), RA 205.0–224.6°, Dec 36.2–42.2°, z 0.745–0.860, largest member separation 742 Mpc. Its CHMS uses spheres of radius half the mean minimum-spanning-tree edge, 30.4 Mpc; the members are frozen in r155_templates.json.

On DESI, a sightline's path runs where Mg II lies redward of the quasar's Lyα emission (the DESI catalogue does not search the forest) and more than 5,000 km s−1 below the quasar, and its intersection with the hull is computed exactly. On the 21,789 absorbers both catalogues hold, DESI EW2796 / Zhu & Ménard REW = 1.03, flat in EW, so the cut W2796 > 0.3 Å is on the SDSS scale. DR1's Mg II detection rate is set mostly by the quasar's flux, and removing SDSS quasars leaves the fainter ones where SDSS observed, so dN/dz is measured per stratum (TSNR2 tertile × 12 g-flux bins × 4 quasar-redshift bins) on all independent sightlines, and the expected count is the sum over the hull's sightlines of path times its stratum's dN/dz. The all-sky check in the slice gives 7,844 expected against 7,864 observed. The hull holds 8 absorbers against 8.86 expected, δ = Nobs/Nexp − 1 = −0.10. The same hull moved rigidly to about 150 places in the SDSS footprint gives a spread of 0.341 at this depth, against Poisson's 0.336: the clustering beyond Poisson is small, a lognormal of width 0.058.

3.2 The member overdensity

The injections are conditioned on the value our estimator gives the Arc, not on a published number our estimator might not reproduce. A group's member overdensity is all slice absorbers in its CHMS over the SDSS expectation in CHMS ∩ slice, minus 1, where the SDSS expectation is the path on the Zhu & Ménard searched sightlines times one measured slice dN/dz per class (BOSS, DR7-only; the classes reproduce the slice's 4,752). On the Arc it gives 1.32 (48 absorbers against 20.7), the published 1.3.

3.3 Injection and recovery

The Arc's CHMS shape is moved rigidly to 200 places in the SDSS footprint: more than 25° from the Arc, with an SDSS expectation 0.75 to 1.33 times the Arc's, and holding fewer than 10 members of any real slice group of 40 or more (14 independent patches more than 20° apart). At each place and each amplitude Atrue ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.3, 1.6, 2, 2.5, 3, 4}, Poisson(Atrue × SDSS expectation) absorbers are added uniformly per unit SDSS path in the shape, into the real catalogue, so the background keeps its real clustering, coverage and chance groups with no model of any of them, and linkage is re-run. The recovered group is the one of 20 or more members holding the most injected absorbers (at Atrue = 0, the most members inside the shape). It is Arc-like if it has 30 to 70 members, a largest separation 0.5 to 2 times the Arc's, and a member overdensity within ±0.3 of the Arc's 1.32. Three shapes are injected: H, uniform in the Arc's hull (the reading under test); S, uniform only in the 30.4 Mpc member spheres (a clumpy arc); and L, the hull of 60.8 Mpc spheres (larger, diffuse). Shape H is also run with SDSS placement weighted by the quasar's g magnitude (seven classes from DR16Q, each with its own slice dN/dz), conditioned on the Arc's value under that model (1.03). That is 8,800 injections.

3.4 What DESI would see

For each Arc-like recovery, two numbers are measured on real DESI data. frec is the share of the recovered hull's DESI expectation that lies inside the injected structure, with both rotated back onto the Arc's own position, so that the DESI weights are those of the Arc's actual 3,822 sightlines and their strata. bg, the chance part, is DESI's own Nobs/Nexp − 1 in the recovered hull at the injection site (the injected absorbers are not in DESI), pooled over the Arc-like recoveries of each hypothesis. A real Arc recovered this way predicts the hull's count as Poisson(8.86 (1 + bg + Atrue frec)) times the lognormal clustering scatter of Section 3.1, and the 8 observed are compared with it at count level, as P(N ≤ 8).

3.5 Inference and the decision rule

The likelihood of the Arc's member overdensity under each Atrue is the share of places that give an Arc-like recovery. With a flat prior on the grid, the real reading's prediction is the mixture over Atrue > 0 of each recovery's prediction, weighted by that likelihood; chance is Atrue = 0. Errors on P come from a block bootstrap over the 14 independent patches. The rule, fixed with the plan before any injection ran: an exclusion is claimed only if the real reading's P ≤ 0.023 (2σ) for shape H, and only for the shapes that give it; otherwise the claim is that the test cannot distinguish.

3.6 Method check and disclosure

Before any injection, the plan alone (ga_rev_plan.md) went to a fresh, context-free referee model as a method check. It returned “fixable” with seven points, all closed before the injections ran: compare at count level with clustering scatter, not with a Gaussian; measure the chance part per hypothesis on Arc-like recoveries rather than subtract a fixed baseline; scan the DESI-to-SDSS bias ratio; fix minimum recovery counts, count independent places and bootstrap the errors, extending the grid to check the likelihood at the top; add a completeness-weighted injection; claim an exclusion only for the shapes that give one, with a larger diffuse shape added; and exclude neighbouring real groups from the places and track merges. Disclosure: the DESI count was known (from the first version) when the plan was written. The plan, the Arc-like window and the decision rule were fixed before any injection was run.

4.1 The primary shape

To come out of the linkage looking like the Arc, a structure uniform in its hull needs Atrue of about 0.5 to 2 (median 1.3; the likelihood peaks at 28 per cent of places at Atrue = 1 and is zero at 4). The hull the algorithm then draws is offset from the structure that seeded it: a median 54 per cent of its DESI path lies inside it, and the injected absorbers are a median 30 per cent of its members. So a real Arc predicts

δ = +0.62  (68 %: +0.25 to +1.05)(1)

on the new sightlines, not +1.3. The observed −0.10 is 1.3σ below it (P = 0.10, bootstrap 0.09 to 0.11) and 0.5σ below chance (an Arc-like group recovered with nothing injected carries +0.15 on DESI; the real slice’s own Arc-like groups carry +0.20). The data favour chance by a factor of 2.7, which does not decide. By the rule of Section 3.5 this is a result that cannot distinguish the two readings.

4.2 The other shapes

No shape reaches 2σ. A clumpy Arc, excess only in its member spheres, mostly relabels a real group (a median 28 members drawn from one) and predicts only +0.26, which chance cannot be told from (likelihood ratio 0.91). A larger diffuse shape and a completeness-weighted injection give 1.2 and 1.1σ.

5Checks

Window, prior, EW cut. A wider Arc-like window (±0.5 in member overdensity) gives P = 0.11; a prior ∝ 1/Atrue gives P = 0.15; W > 0.6 Å (8 against 8.40) gives P = 0.13.

DESI against SDSS. A structure’s overdensity in DESI’s absorbers need not equal its overdensity in Zhu & Ménard’s. With the ratio r = δDESI/δSDSS scanned, P = 0.28, 0.19, 0.14, 0.10 and 0.08 at r = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1 and 1.25: no plausible r reaches 2σ.

Monte Carlo and the grid. The block bootstrap gives P between 0.09 and 0.11 for the primary shape. The likelihood is zero at Atrue = 4 for H and L, so the grid does not truncate them; for S it is still rising at 4, which only lowers that shape’s prediction further.

Measured against the published value. The first version’s comparison stands as arithmetic: the hull’s fitted amplitude, A = −0.23 (95 per cent: −0.90 to +0.63), excludes 1.3 at 3.2σ. It is not a test of reality, because a real structure recovered by this recipe does not carry 1.3 on new sightlines.

6Discussion

The gap between “3.2σ below 1.3” and “1.3σ below a real Arc” is the selection shrinkage, measured here on the real catalogue rather than on mocks: the member overdensity a linkage group carries is roughly twice the excess its seeding structure shows on new sightlines through the hull the algorithm draws. About nine expected absorbers cannot separate +0.6 from chance (+0.15), so this count does not decide between the reading that the Arc is real (Lopez, Clowes & Williger 2022; Lopez & Clowes 2025) and the reading that it is a pattern among ordinary absorbers (Sawala et al. 2025; Sawala & Teeriaho 2025). What it does establish is how large a real Arc’s signal on independent sightlines would be, and so what an independent test needs: the published amplitude is the wrong yardstick by a factor of about two.

7Limitations

  • No filament-shaped injection. The real-Arc prediction rests on uniform and sphere-clumped shapes; a thin filament along the arc, the shape its discoverers describe, was not injected. “Cannot tell” is shown for those shapes, and the DR2 forecast below should be redone with a filament. This is the finding check’s strongest objection (Section 8).
  • One DESI release and about nine expected absorbers: the test’s power is the limit, not its systematics.
  • The SDSS completeness is modelled by class (and, as a variant, by quasar magnitude), not per spectrum.
  • The rebuilt Arc has 46 members, the published one 44; the template is ours, frozen from the published recipe.
  • The Big Ring and the Giant Ring are not tested: neither paper gives a member list or a reproducible membership recipe.

8Review status

Every verdict is from a fresh, context-free referee model given only the text, doing its own literature search, and answering in a fixed verdict line (protocol in the public repository’s blind-review.md). The first version passed its finding check on 2 November (“the findings are confirmed novel and sound if the calculations hold”), with selection shrinkage as its strongest objection. On 3 November a re-check, run on the email to the discoverers after the page’s title was reworded, returned “novel but not sound”: the 3.2σ compares against a member-selected overdensity a real structure would not reproduce on new sightlines, so the test cannot discriminate a real Arc from chance. This revision is its one answer. Its plan passed a method check as “fixable” (Section 3.6), and the revised finding passed the finding check: “the findings are confirmed novel and sound if the calculations hold”, with the strongest objection that no filament was injected (Section 7). The revised title and abstract were checked once more, by another fresh referee, and passed again with the same line; it called the test weak at this depth (even a perfect measurement of nine expected absorbers separates +0.62 from +0.15 by only about 1.4σ) and the DR2 forecast optimistic, since it assumes the count holds and the expected count carries no systematic error. The closest work the referees found: Sawala & Teeriaho 2025 (arXiv:2505.11072); also arXiv:2502.03515, 2201.06875, 2402.07591, 2604.17534, 2504.14940, 1211.6256, 1306.1700, 1603.03260 and 2412.15383.

The note was briefly withdrawn on 3 November, because its plan had said a test that cannot distinguish the two readings would be withdrawn. That rule governs what is claimed, and the claim here is no exclusion; a sound result that does not decide is published as its interval.

9What would settle it

DESI DR2, with about twice the sightlines, brings the count’s error from about 0.34 to about 0.24 in δ: a real Arc’s +0.6 would then sit about 2σ from chance, so DR2 could begin to tell the readings apart if the count held near −0.10. A filament-shaped injection should be run first, since it sets the prediction DR2 would test. The Big Ring is the next structure to test once its member list is public.

10Reproducibility

All data are public: the Zhu & Ménard catalogues and searched-quasar lists from the JHU-SDSS absorber archive, the DESI DR1 Mg II value-added catalogue and quasar catalogue from data.desi.lbl.gov, and SDSS DR16Q from the SDSS science archive. The analysis is in the public research repository. The template and DESI count: r155_template.py rebuilds and freezes the Arc, r155_sightlines.py builds the DESI sightlines and removes the SDSS ones, r155_core.py computes paths, completeness strata and counts, r155_run.py and r155_measure.py run the nulls and the measurement, with the cosmology and matching in r155_common.py. The revision: garev_inject.py runs the injections and garev_infer.py the inference, from the plan ga_rev_plan.md. Every number is in results-R155.md; the revised finding and its verdict are in finding-ga-rev.md, and the first version with its verdicts in finding.md.

Methods and code. How this paper was chosen, refereed and recorded: the methods note. Scripts, results and referee verdicts: github.com/babyteam-hq/cosmo-discovery.
