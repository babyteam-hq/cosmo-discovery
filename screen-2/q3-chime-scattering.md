# Proposed test: do intervening galaxy halos scatter fast radio bursts? CHIME/FRB Catalog 2 scattering times against foreground galaxy density

Question: how much of fast-radio-burst temporal scattering comes from intervening halos rather than hosts and the Milky Way? Cross-correlate the scattering times of CHIME/FRB Catalog 2 bursts with Legacy Survey galaxy overdensity in redshift slices, and compare sightlines passing within the virial radius of nearby galaxies with matched controls. The claim: the cross-correlation amplitude (or an upper limit) and the ratio of median scattering time near and away from foreground halos, with intervals.

Data: CHIME/FRB Catalog 2 (January 2026; about 4,500 bursts from about 3,600 sources, scattering times and localisations of 10-15 arcmin) and its injection-based selection function; DESI Legacy Survey DR9/DR10 galaxies with photometric redshifts; the z0MGS/GLADE+ nearby-galaxy catalogue for the halo-intersection test; NE2001 for the Galactic scattering contribution.

Method: fixed before computing: the sample (one burst per source, |b| > 20 deg, scattering measured or a stated upper limit, censored values handled by survival analysis), scattering scaled to 600 MHz, the redshift slices and the impact-parameter cut. Statistics: the angular cross-correlation of log scattering with galaxy overdensity; the near/far median ratio with a censored-data estimator. Nulls: rotated galaxy maps, stars, Galactic-latitude splits, selection-function-weighted randoms; DM is controlled for as a covariate.

Expected precision: likely an upper limit at 10-20 % of the sensitivity of the published DM x galaxy cross-correlation; a detection only if halos dominate the scattering.
