# Proposed test: the galaxy bias of binary-black-hole merger hosts from GWTC-4 sky maps crossed with the DESI Legacy Survey galaxies

Question: in what kind of halos do binary black holes merge? The angular cross-correlation of the GWTC-4 binary-black-hole localisation probability maps with DESI Legacy Survey galaxy overdensity in redshift slices matched to each event's luminosity-distance posterior gives the bias of GW hosts, b_GW, with its interval: high bias means massive halos and old, metal-poor progenitors; low bias, young star-forming galaxies.

Data: GWTC-4.0 (O4a) and GWTC-3 public sky maps (HEALPix posteriors) and distance posteriors from GWOSC; DESI Legacy Imaging Survey DR9/DR10 galaxies with photometric redshifts (and DESI DR1 spectroscopic where available); the LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA selection-function injections.

Method: fixed before computing: the event set (BBH, 90 % area < 1000 deg^2), redshift slices, the galaxy sample and its bias (from its auto-correlation). Statistic: the cross-power of the stacked GW probability maps with the galaxy overdensity in the matched slice, fitted for b_GW with the LCDM matter power at Planck cosmology. Errors from rotating the GW maps and from the injection set placed at random; nulls from distance-mismatched slices.

Expected precision: about 150 BBH events; b_GW to about +-1.0 at present, enough to rule out b_GW > 3 or to see a signal if hosts are massive ellipticals.
