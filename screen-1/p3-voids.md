# Proposed test: the CMB-lensing profile of cosmic voids from ACT DR6 against the LCDM prediction for the same voids

Question: is the stacked CMB-lensing convergence of large voids at 0.2 < z < 0.8 consistent in amplitude with the LCDM prediction computed from the voids' own galaxy density profiles, measured at ACT's resolution? The amplitude A = observed/predicted with its interval.

Data: the public void catalogues from BOSS DR12 (Mao et al. 2017; the REVOLVER/ZOBOV catalogues of Nadathur et al.) and the DESI Legacy Survey photometric voids if released; ACT DR6 lensing convergence with its 400 simulations; Planck PR4 lensing for comparison.

Method: voids in 3 radius bins, fixed before stacking; stack kappa in rescaled radius r/R_v; the template is the voids' galaxy density profile divided by the sample's linear bias (from the catalogue's own clustering), projected; fit A per bin. Covariance from the 400 simulations; random-position and rotated-map nulls; the same stack on Planck PR4 for consistency with earlier Planck results.

Expected precision: of order 5-10 thousand voids in the ACT footprint; void lensing at 5-8 sigma; A to about 15 %.
