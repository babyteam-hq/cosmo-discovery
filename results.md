arXiv:2408.00726) is not eRASS1: it uses eeHIFLUGCS (313 clusters) with velocity dispersions, L_X-σ_v and Y_SZ-σ_v, and
finds the maximum H0 variation toward (l, b) = (295 ± 71, −30 ± 71) at 3.64σ; exposure is not a variable there (σ_v does not
depend on X-ray depth, and L_X comes from the flux-limited ROSAT sample). Migkas+2021 (A&A 649, A151, ten relations, 570
clusters) give (280, −15), 9 % in H0, 5.4σ, and examine X-ray absorption, the Zone of Avoidance and bulk flows as systematics,
not survey exposure or depth. He, Migkas et al. 2025 (A&A 702, A268, arXiv:2504.01745, FLAMINGO lightcones with the same
selection) lower that to 3.2σ (scatter inflates the apparent amplitude; >80 % of each lightcone's anisotropy is noise) and
name eRASS1 as the next application. Migkas et al. 2024 (A&A 688, A107, arXiv:2401.17297) is eROSITA's temperature
cross-calibration against Chandra and XMM (eROSITA T lower, more so for hotter clusters), not an isotropy test; Migkas's 2023
conference claim that eRASS1 "confirms" the anisotropy at z < 0.2 has no refereed paper behind it; the 2025 eRASS1
scaling-relation paper (arXiv:2511.14356, 3,061 clusters, selection function from eROSITA sky simulations) runs no
direction test. None of them notes the geometry that `erass1_lxt.py` turns on: Migkas's axis is 15° from the south ecliptic
pole (276, −30) and Pandya's 16°, and the ecliptic poles are where every scanning all-sky X-ray survey (RASS and eRASS alike)
is deepest, so an exposure-driven selection residual in a flux-limited L-T normalisation has the right direction and the
right sign (deeper exposure admits fainter clusters at a given T, lowering A, which reads as a lower H0). Whether the RASS
exposure pattern can do to eeHIFLUGCS (bright, f > 5e-12) what eRASS1's does to the eRASS1 T sample is not tested here; it
is the obvious next test and would need the RASS exposure map at the 313 positions.
