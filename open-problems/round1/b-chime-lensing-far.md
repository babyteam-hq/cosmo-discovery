# Proposed test: the false-alarm rate of gravitationally lensed fast-radio-burst candidates in CHIME/FRB data

Question: claims of lensed FRB candidates in CHIME/FRB data (an echo found by the burst autocorrelation statistic) have been used to infer that compact objects (intermediate-mass or primordial black holes) make up a few percent of dark matter. How many such "lensed" detections does the same statistic produce on bursts that cannot be lensed, and so what is the measured false-alarm rate and the surviving compact-object fraction, with an interval?

Data: the public CHIME/FRB Catalog 1 and Catalog 2 baseband or waterfall data products (whatever the catalogue releases at the needed time resolution), including repeating sources; the published candidate list and statistic.

Method: fixed before computing: the statistic and threshold exactly as published; the null sets: (i) bursts from repeaters at times when the claimed lens delay would be inconsistent between bursts of the same source, (ii) waterfalls with time-scrambled or phase-randomised structure preserving each burst's power spectrum, (iii) injected unlensed synthetic bursts with real noise. The false-alarm rate per burst is the fraction of null bursts above threshold; the compact-object fraction is recomputed with the measured rate, as a posterior or an upper limit.

Expected precision: thousands of null realisations; the false-alarm rate to a few tenths of a percent per burst, enough to test whether a 3-4 sigma candidate survives.
