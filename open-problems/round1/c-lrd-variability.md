# Proposed test: optical variability of low-redshift "little red dot" analogues against matched quasars, from ZTF light curves

Question: are the local analogues of JWST's "little red dots" (a sample of about 240 SDSS objects with V-shaped spectral energy distributions and broad lines, identified in 2026) variable like ordinary broad-line AGN? The ratio of their optical variability amplitude to that of quasars matched in luminosity and redshift tests whether their broad-line continuum comes from an accreting black hole seen directly, or is reprocessed or dominated by dense gas or stellar light.

Data: the published SDSS local-LRD sample; SDSS DR16Q for controls; ZTF DR23+ forced or PSF light curves (IRSA), g and r, 2018-2025.

Method: fixed before computing: the sample, the control matching (bolometric luminosity, redshift, magnitude), the statistic (the structure-function amplitude at a rest-frame lag of ~100 days, and the excess variance), the host-light correction (aperture photometry with the host fraction from the SDSS spectrum decomposition). Interval by bootstrap over objects; nulls from non-variable stars and galaxies matched in magnitude.

Expected precision: ~240 objects against ~2,000 controls; the variability ratio to about 10-15 %.
