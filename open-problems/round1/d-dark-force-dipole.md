# Proposed test: a dark-sector equivalence-principle violation from the relative dipole between two DESI galaxy samples

Question: the preference of CMB+BAO for a "negative" neutrino mass can be mimicked by a long-range dark-sector force that violates the equivalence principle between dark matter and baryons. Such a force leaves a distinctive, CMB-independent signature: an imaginary (odd-multipole) dipole in the cross-power spectrum of two galaxy samples with different biases, in the squeezed limit. Measure its amplitude beta from DESI DR1 with an interval, and compare with the beta that would explain the CMB+BAO preference.

Data: DESI DR1 LRG and ELG catalogues (and BGS), their random catalogues and the public EZmock/Abacus mocks.

Method: fixed before computing: the tracer pairs (LRG x ELG in the overlapping redshift range, BGS x LRG as the check), the estimator (the imaginary part of the cross-power dipole in the plane-parallel and wide-angle-corrected forms), the k range, the wide-angle and relativistic-effect templates (gravitational redshift and Doppler terms also produce odd multipoles; they are modelled and marginalised), and the likelihood. Covariance from the mocks; nulls from the same estimator on two random halves of one tracer (no relative bias, no signal).

Expected precision: beta to about +-0.01 to 0.02, against CMB+BAO's beta ~ 0.004 +- 0.002: a consistency test rather than a detection, unless the force is larger.
